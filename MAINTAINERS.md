# ThunderID Maintainers

The people who maintain ThunderID. Maintainers review designs, review code, shape the roadmap, ship releases, and keep the community healthy. Decisions are made by consensus among maintainers.

## Core Maintainers

Core Maintainers have write/merge access across the entire repository and are responsible for stewardship of the project’s long-term roadmap and architectural consistency. Core Maintainers are expected to remain active, participate in design discussions, and provide timely reviews.

In addition to these responsibilities, the Core Maintainers collectively serve as the project’s Security Team, handling security-related issues, coordinating responsible disclosures, and ensuring that patches and advisories are managed appropriately.

## **How to Become a Maintainer**

ThunderID is security-critical infrastructure, so the bar for maintainership is deliberately high. It is a bar on demonstrated competence, not on affiliation: anyone who meets it is welcome, and growing the maintainer group beyond its founding organization is an explicit goal of the project.

### **What is expected before someone is considered**

A candidate is expected to have demonstrated, over a sustained period:

* A clear working understanding of the ThunderID codebase and its architecture.  
* A solid grasp of IAM concepts and the security implications of changes within an identity system.  
* Substantial, non-trivial contributions to the project.  
* High-quality reviews, particularly on security-sensitive changes, and active participation in design and architectural discussions.  
* Willingness to serve as a long-term maintainer.

These expectations scale with the role. An Area Maintainer is expected to have demonstrated depth in the area they would own; a Core Maintainer is expected to work across the whole project and to take on architectural and security stewardship.

### **How a maintainer is proposed and approved**

1. **Proposal.** An existing active maintainer proposes the candidate by opening a pull request that adds them to the appropriate table above. The maintainer who opens the pull request is the candidate's sponsor. The candidate confirms, in a comment on the pull request, their willingness to take on the role.  
2. **Announcement.** The proposal is announced on the project's public communication channels, so the community has the opportunity to comment. The pull request stays open for community comment for at least two weeks.  
3. **Decision.** The proposal is approved when an absolute majority of the active maintainers have approved the pull request. Any active Core Maintainer may veto the proposal; a veto must include a written explanation on the pull request and block the addition. Approvals and vetoes are recorded on the pull request. If the proposal has neither reached an absolute majority nor been vetoed within four weeks, it may be closed.   
4. **Enactment.** Once approved, the pull request is merged and the new maintainer's repository access is granted.

## **How Maintainers Are Removed or Moved to Emeritus Status**

Being a maintainer is not a permanent title. A maintainer may be moved to emeritus status when:

* The maintainer violates the OpenWallet Foundation Code of Conduct in a way that warrants removal.  
* The maintainer becomes inactive, as defined below.  
* The maintainer resigns by initializing a self request.  
* Other circumstances arise, at the discretion of the active maintainers.

### **Inactivity**

A maintainer is considered inactive when they have made no substantial contribution to the project such as merged changes, substantive code reviews, active participation in design and architectural discussions for three consecutive months. The Core Maintainers are responsible for identifying inactivity and will notify the maintainer before any action is taken. A maintainer who states an intention to continue, or who have already notified the Core Maintainers regarding long-term leave such as parental or medical leave, is not treated as inactive. If inactivity continues after notification, the Core Maintainers may open a proposal to move the maintainer to emeritus status.

### **Process**

Moving a maintainer to emeritus status follows the same mechanics as adding one: a pull request is opened against this file, approved by an absolute majority of active maintainers, and subject to veto by any active Core Maintainer with a written explanation. In the case of a voluntary resignation, the pull request may be merged once the resigning maintainer has confirmed it.

A maintainer who has been moved to emeritus status may return to active status only through the same process used to add a new maintainer, and must meet the same expectations at the time they return.
