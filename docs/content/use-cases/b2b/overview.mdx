# Understand It

A company signs up for your product. Its office manager creates an account and the company in the same visit, then
invites the team. A month later a larger customer signs up, but its IT department will not hand out yet another
password: its employees must sign in through the company's own identity provider. Then a consultant starts working with
both companies at once.

To each company, your product is simply the tool its people use. To your backend, every request now raises questions
that a consumer application never had to answer:

- Which company is this person acting for right now?
- How did they come to belong to that company, and who decided it?
- What are they allowed to do there, and only there?
- Who runs each company's team: your staff, or the company's own administrator?

Handling those questions is what it takes to build identity for a B2B SaaS application, and the sign-in screen is the
smallest part of it.

## The Problem

A first version usually treats every user as an individual with a `company` field on their profile. As customers
arrive, the same challenges surface whatever the product is:

- **Self-service company onboarding.** A new customer expects to create their company and its first administrator in
  one sign-up, without a sales call or a ticket to your team.
- **One person, several companies.** Consultants, agencies, and parent-company staff need one account that can act for
  more than one customer, with different permissions in each.
- **Each company's own sign-in.** Larger customers require their employees to sign in through the company's identity
  provider (IdP), the system such as Okta or Microsoft Entra ID that already holds their workforce accounts.
- **Delegated administration.** Each customer wants its own administrator to add people, form teams, and grant access,
  without filing requests with you and without ever seeing another customer's people.
- **Isolation on every request.** Every API call has to know which company it acts for, so one customer's data never
  leaks into another's, even when the same person belongs to both.
- **One deployment for every customer.** Roles, sign-in journeys, and the application itself should be defined once
  and reach every company, including the ones that sign up tomorrow.

## How It Works

Solving this puts a dedicated **identity layer** between your application and the people who use it, and models each
customer company as an **organization** inside it. A platform organization, owned by your team, defines your
application, its roles, and its sign-in journeys once, and shares them with every customer organization beneath it.
Each customer organization holds its own people and its own settings, such as which identity provider its employees
sign in with.

When someone signs in, the identity layer decides which organization the sign-in lands in, checks that the person
belongs there, and issues a signed **token**: a piece of data your application presents to its APIs, signed so the
APIs can detect any change to it. The token names the organization and lists only the roles the person holds in it.

The organization is decided once, at sign-in, and travels in the token. Every API call after that knows which company
it acts for without asking again, and a person who belongs to two companies holds a separate token for each.

## What You Can Build

<ProductName /> covers each stage of a customer company's life on your platform. Every journey below runs as a
[flow](../../guides/flows/what-are-flows.mdx), the sequence of screens and checks one journey runs, which you build from
a template in a visual designer and change without redeploying your application.

- **Company sign-up.** One sign-up creates the person's account, creates the company's organization, and makes that
  person its first administrator. You choose whether the account lives inside the new organization or stays on the
  platform so it can join other companies later. An **HTTP Request** step can call your own services during sign-up,
  for example to check billing details or to tell your CRM about the new customer. See
  [Let Companies Sign Up](./journeys/organization-sign-up.mdx).
- **Invitations.** An organization's administrator invites people by email, whether or not they already have an account
  on your platform. Accepting adds them to the organization with the roles the administrator picked, and creates the
  account first when there is none. See [Invite People In](./journeys/invite-members.mdx).
- **Enterprise identity per organization.** Each company can connect its own OpenID Connect identity provider. Its
  employees' accounts are created inside the organization the first time they sign in, so nobody has to add them in
  advance, and [account linking](../../guides/identity-providers/link-accounts.mdx) joins a federated sign-in to an
  account that already exists. See [Plug In an Enterprise IdP](./journeys/enterprise-sso.mdx).
- **Sign-in that knows the organization.** One shared sign-in journey serves every company. A person reaches their
  organization by its handle, through a link your application sends, or by choosing after sign-in when they belong to
  several. Each organization can point the shared journey at its own identity provider. See
  [Switch Organizations](./journeys/switch-organizations.mdx).
- **Roles defined once, assigned per organization.** Roles and their permissions live in the platform organization and
  are shared with every company. Each company assigns them to its own people, and a token carries only the roles held
  in the organization it names.
- **Delegated administration.** Management permissions are confined to the organization a token names, so a company's
  administrator manages users, groups, and role assignments in that company only. See
  [Let Org Admins Manage Teams](./journeys/delegated-administration.mdx).
- **Policy-based sharing.** Applications, roles, flows, identity provider connections, users, and agents can be shared
  with chosen organizations or all of them, with rules for what each organization may change. See
  [Resource Sharing Across Organization Units](../../key-concepts/resource-sharing.mdx).
- **Account recovery, consent, and protection.** Password recovery runs from the **Email Link Password Recovery** and
  **SMS OTP Password Recovery** templates. A [consent](../../guides/consent.mdx) step asks people before their data is
  shared, and [account protection](../../guides/account-protection/overview.mdx) locks or suspends accounts that need
  it.
- **SDK components.** The React SDK adds an organization switcher, user, group, and role management views, and
  invitation screens to your own application. See [B2B SDK Components](./sdk-components.mdx).

:::tip Not Your Pattern?
- Users are individual consumers who each own their account: see [Secure Consumer Access to Your Application](../b2c/index.mdx).
- Users are autonomous agents or machine-to-machine clients: see [Identity for AI Agents](../ai-agents/index.mdx).
:::

None of this locks you into a proprietary protocol: sign-in runs on standard
[OAuth2 and OpenID Connect](../../guides/protocols/oauth-oidc/index.mdx), so your team's existing tooling and knowledge
already apply. By the end of this section, companies sign themselves up, bring in their people by invitation or through
their own identity provider, and manage their own teams, all from one shared application.
